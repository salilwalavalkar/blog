## 🐦 X / Twitter

Your coding agent runs 10,000 tests before lunch. A rocket runs one eight-second test, then someone drives the hard drive back from the desert. AI can't prompt its way past atoms. https://salilwalavalkar.github.io/blog/posts/2026-06-03-hardware-bottleneck-ai-cant-fix/

## 🧵 Threads

AI agents are eating software because code can be tested in seconds. Hardware gets one eight-second rocket test after years of work, and that data is basically the whole company.

Meanwhile, software observability is designed to aggregate everything into P99s and forget it a week later. Turns out "cattle, not pets" doesn't work when you own exactly two aircraft.

And LLMs don't know physics. They know English descriptions of physics. Not ideal for anything with a person inside it. https://salilwalavalkar.github.io/blog/posts/2026-06-03-hardware-bottleneck-ai-cant-fix/

## 🐘 Mastodon

Notes on SE Daily's episode with Jason Hoch of Nominal. Hardware test data breaks software observability habits: tens of thousands of kHz-to-MHz sensors, no downsampling allowed, schemas that drift when a technician forgets a config value, and video that must line up frame by frame with time series. AI can't shorten the loop because physical tests and supply chains set the pace. The real fix is boring: cataloging, tagging and lineage. https://salilwalavalkar.github.io/blog/posts/2026-06-03-hardware-bottleneck-ai-cant-fix/
