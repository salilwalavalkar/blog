## The 30-Second Reality Check

The "mind palace" is the [method of loci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_loci): a memory trick from ancient Greece where you stash facts in rooms of a place you already know. [Sherlock](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1475582/) didn't invent it. He just gave it better lighting and a dramatic swelling soundtrack.

## Explain With Pictures

```
  What TV shows you                 What it actually is

  +---------------------+           Front door  -> milk
  |  GENIUS STARES INTO |           Hallway     -> eggs
  |  THE MIDDLE DISTANCE|   ==>     Kitchen     -> the dentist at 3pm
  |  *dramatic music*   |           Bedroom     -> that API key you
  +---------------------+                          really shouldn't memorize

  Superpower                        A hash map keyed by furniture
```

The trick works because your brain is bad at random lists and great at places. So you cheat: key the list by a place.

## 3 Hot Takes & Quotes

**1. It's an index, not more storage.**

> a mind palace is a place so well known to you that you can store large bits of information with relative ease. — me, 2019

Close, past me, but the palace doesn't add capacity. It adds _lookup_. Your brain already has the data; it just has terrible retrieval. The palace is a database index built from the floor plan of your childhood home. Every engineer who's sped up a slow query by adding an index has already built one.

**2. Television ruins every good technique by making it look effortless.**

> Sherlock Holmes withdraws into his mind palace, seeking clues and mental cues to help solve the Hounds of Baskerville Case. — me, 2019

On screen it takes eight seconds and a slow zoom. In reality, memory athletes spend months building and rehearsing their palaces. It's the same lie as the hacker typing furiously into a green terminal: the montage skips the practice, and the practice is the whole point.

**3. "How I discovered it" is the most honest part.**

> How i discovered the Mind Palace — me, 2019, describing a technique older than the printing press

I "discovered" it the way most people did: from a TV show. Fine. Most good engineering ideas reach us the same way, rediscovered decades later in a conference talk with a fancy new name.

## The Post-Mortem / Verdict

It works, it's ancient, and it's a cache with a nice floor plan. Use it for talks, lists and names at meetups. Don't use it for passwords. That's what a password manager is for, and it doesn't need you to imagine a hallway.
